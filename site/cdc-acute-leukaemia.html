Chronic disseminated candidiasis in acute leukaemia: a full-text-verified literature-derived case series of 10 cases from 7 sources (English and Chinese literature)

CASE SERIES · DRAFT v0.3 · AWAITING HUMAN REVIEW

⚠ AI-assisted preprint — not peer-reviewed, not medical advice. Search, screening, full-text retrieval, extraction, analysis and drafting were performed by an AI agent under investigator supervision. All extracted values require verification against source papers before this draft is declared final. Full audit trail in the linked GitHub repository.

Abstract

Background: Chronic disseminated candidiasis (CDC; formerly hepatosplenic candidiasis) is a distinctive invasive fungal infection occurring predominantly in patients with acute leukaemia during or after prolonged neutropenia. Evidence remains limited to single case reports.

Methods: We searched Europe PMC and PubMed (2000–2026) for case reports of CDC in acute leukaemia. Of 48 unique records screened, 19 met the case definition; following a full-text availability gate, the final cohort comprised the 7 sources with retrievable full text (10 cases), each analysed individually at full-text level, including a Chinese-language series of 3 cases analysed in the original language. Case-level data were extracted into a structured dataset and analysed descriptively following CARE guidelines.

Results: Reported ages ranged 2–47 years (median 18; age reported in 9 of 10 cases): 4 paediatric, 5 adult. Leukaemia was ALL in 5 cases and AML in 5 (including APL, AML-M3 and AML-M5b in complete remission). Candida species were identified in 8 of 10 cases: Candida tropicalis in 4, Candida albicans in 3 and Candida parapsilosis in 1 — by tissue or aspirate culture, repeated blood cultures (including one pan-azole-resistant isolate), and by tissue or blood metagenomic next-generation sequencing (mNGS) in culture-negative cases. IRIS complicated 3 cases; all resolved with adjuvant corticosteroids plus continued antifungals. Surgical intervention was used in 4 cases (splenectomy in 3, incision and drainage in 1), all with reported success. No deaths occurred; 8 of 10 cases were reported resolved or in leukaemia remission, and 1 was lost to follow-up.

Conclusions: In this full-text-verified, bilingual case series, CDC in acute leukaemia — including culture-negative, NGS/PCR-diagnosed and azole-resistant cases — had favourable reported outcomes with prolonged combination antifungal therapy, adjuvant corticosteroids for IRIS, and surgical salvage for refractory disease. mNGS in blood or tissue enabled species-level diagnosis where cultures were negative. Reporting heterogeneity remains high; a systematic registry is needed.

Introduction

Chronic disseminated candidiasis (CDC) is a subacute invasive candidal infection involving liver, spleen and other organs, classically described in patients with acute leukaemia recovering from prolonged chemotherapy-induced neutropenia, with an estimated incidence below 5% of treated acute leukaemia patients. Persistent fever after neutrophil recovery, negative blood cultures and characteristic target-like hepatic and splenic lesions form the typical presentation. Individual case reports describe a wide spectrum of presentations (muscular and subcutaneous abscesses), diagnostic approaches (PET/CT, mannan antigen, beta-D-glucan, mNGS) and treatments (prolonged echinocandin or azole therapy, adjuvant corticosteroids for IRIS, splenectomy). Because no single centre accumulates enough cases, each report contributes one fragment of the clinical picture. We hypothesised that aggregating published case-level data, restricted to sources verifiable at full text, would yield a stronger descriptive evidence base than any individual report.

Methods

This systematic aggregation of published case reports followed CARE guidelines. We searched Europe PMC and PubMed (E-utilities) on 30 September 2026 (exact query strings in the project search log) for CDC/hepatosplenic candidiasis in leukaemia, 2000–2026. Of 48 unique records, 19 sources met the case definition. A full-text availability gate was then applied: only sources whose full text could be retrieved and analysed (PubMed Central deposit or publisher open access) entered the final cohort — 7 sources contributing 10 cases. Each included source's full text was read and case-level data extracted independently (demographics, leukaemia subtype and phase, prophylaxis, Candida species and identification method, organs, diagnostic basis, antifungal regimen, surgery, IRIS, outcome). Fields unavailable in full text were marked UNVERIFIED; none were imputed. Descriptive statistics were computed in Python; no inferential pooling was attempted given heterogeneity and incomplete reporting.

Results

The final cohort comprised 10 cases from 7 full-text-analysed sources (Table 1). Ages ranged 2–47 years (median 18): 4 paediatric and 5 adult. Leukaemia subtype was ALL in 5 cases and AML in 5 (APL in 1, AML-M3 in 1, AML-M5b in complete remission in 1, CEBPA-mutated AML in 1). Candida species were identified in 8 of 10 cases: C. tropicalis in 4, C. albicans in 3 and C. parapsilosis in 1. Diagnosis was culture-negative in 4 cases; mNGS, tissue PCR/sequencing, mannan antigen or histopathology established the diagnosis. IRIS complicated 3 cases (2 confirmed, 1 suspected); corticosteroids produced defervescence within 72 hours to 3 days where reported, with continued antifungals. Surgery was performed in 4 cases: laparoscopic splenectomy in 3 (all after antifungal failure, including one pan-azole-resistant C. tropicalis) and incision/drainage in 1; fever resolved immediately post-splenectomy where reported. No deaths were reported; 8 of 10 cases were resolved or in leukaemia remission at last follow-up (up to 25 months) and 1 was lost to follow-up.

Discussion

This full-text-verified series makes four practical points. First, microbiological documentation is achievable more often than classical teaching suggests when modern methods are used — species were identified in 8 of 10 cases, including by mNGS and tissue PCR in culture-negative disease — and one documented pan-azole-resistant C. tropicalis infection underlines the value of susceptibility testing. Second, NGS (blood or tissue) enabled species-level diagnosis and diagnosis-driven therapy in culture-negative CDC. Third, IRIS is a recurring complication in children with ALL during immune recovery and responds rapidly to corticosteroids, but diagnosis requires excluding concurrent infection. Fourth, surgical salvage (splenectomy after antifungal failure, or drainage of persistent abscesses) achieved resolution in all 4 surgical cases.

Limitations: This synthesis includes only published, full-text-accessible cases and inherits publication bias favouring successful outcomes (no deaths were reported); the abstract-level screening pool (12 further sources without open full text) was excluded rather than verified; the Chinese-language source was analysed by the AI agent and requires re-verification by a Chinese-reading co-author; and a 2025 systematic review already covers paediatric CDC. All extracted values require author verification before submission.
