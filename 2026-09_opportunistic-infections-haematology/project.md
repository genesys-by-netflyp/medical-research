# Project: Chronic disseminated candidiasis in acute leukaemia — literature-based case series

## One-paragraph summary
Opportunistic fungal infections remain a major cause of morbidity in acute leukaemia. Chronic disseminated candidiasis (CDC, formerly hepatosplenic candidiasis) is a distinctive syndrome occurring during recovery from prolonged neutropenia. This project aggregates published case reports of CDC in patients with acute leukaemia into a structured case series (CARE guidelines), describing demographics, leukaemia subtype, neutropenia, Candida species, diagnostic pathway, treatment, and outcomes — pooled to give stronger descriptive evidence than single reports.

## Case definition
- Population: patients of any age with acute myeloid or lymphoblastic leukaemia (incl. relapsed/transplant contexts)
- Exposure: chronic disseminated candidiasis (chronic candidaemia, hepatosplenic/organ involvement on imaging or histology, typical indolent course)
- Comparator: none (descriptive)
- Outcomes: species distribution, diagnostic delay, antifungal regimen, 30-day/in-hospital mortality, relapse

## Inclusion criteria
- Published as individual case report or small series (≤5 cases per source)
- English or translatable full text; publication year 2000–2026
- Extractable detail: ≥4 of — age/sex, leukaemia type, Candida species, treatment, outcome

## Exclusion criteria
- Narrative reviews, editorials, letters without case-level detail
- Non-leukaemia haematology populations unless the case has acute leukaemia
