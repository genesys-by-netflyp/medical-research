== ARTICLE TITLE ==
Acute 2,4-dichlorophenoxyacetic acid herbicide poisoning in rural India: clinical spectrum, multiorgan toxicity, and outcomes in an eight-patient observational study

== ABSTRACT ==
Acute poisoning with 2,4-dichlorophenoxyacetic acid (2,4-D), a commonly available chlorophenoxy herbicide, represents an emerging toxicological challenge in rural agricultural settings yet remains underreported in intensive care literature. We conducted a retrospective observational study of eight consecutive patients with suspected acute 2,4-D ingestion admitted to a rural tertiary-care center in India to characterize clinical presentation, organ toxicity, management, and outcomes. The cohort demonstrated a wide clinical spectrum, ranging from mild gastrointestinal illness to rapidly progressive multiorgan dysfunction. Severe toxicity was characterized by early hypotension, profound metabolic acidosis, central nervous system depression, rhabdomyolysis, and acute kidney injury requiring renal replacement therapy. Notably, one patient developed electrocardiographic changes and echocardiographic myocardial dysfunction progressing to cardiogenic shock, highlighting potential cardiac involvement that may be underrecognized in phenoxy herbicide poisoning. Management was largely supportive and included hemodynamic stabilization, ventilatory support, urinary alkalinization, and dialysis in selected cases. Overall mortality was 50%, with adverse outcomes associated with early hemodynamic instability and multisystem involvement at presentation. These findings expand the limited clinical literature on 2,4-D toxicity from low-resource settings and emphasize the need for heightened clinical suspicion, early aggressive supportive care, and improved toxicovigilance. Larger prospective studies are warranted to clarify predictors of severity and optimize management strategies for chlorophenoxy herbicide poisoning.

== Introduction ==

Intentional agrochemical ingestion remains a frequent emergency presentation in rural Central India, reflecting both accessibility of pesticides and gaps in mental health resources [1], [2]. In many low- and middle-income settings, these exposures account for a substantial proportion of emergency department visits and intensive care unit admissions. Organophosphate compounds are most frequently implicated; however, herbicides are increasingly accessible and may be associated with severe toxicity and high mortality [3].

2,4-Dichlorophenoxyacetic acid (2,4-D) is a chlorophenoxy herbicide widely used for the control of broad-leaf weeds in agricultural settings. It is available in multiple salt and ester formulations that are easily obtainable in rural markets. Although reports of 2,4-D poisoning from India remain limited, existing literature suggests that moderate-to-severe exposure can result in significant multisystem involvement, including neurological depression, rhabdomyolysis, acute kidney injury, and cardiovascular instability [4], [5].

A major clinical challenge in the management of 2,4-D poisoning is its phenotypic overlap with organophosphate and carbamate toxicity, particularly during early presentation. Features such as miosis, altered sensorium, hypotension, and metabolic acidosis may lead to diagnostic uncertainty, delayed recognition, and inappropriate initial management [6]. In addition, the absence of readily available toxicological assays in many rural centers necessitates reliance on clinical judgment and supportive care strategies.

Given the limited clinical data from rural intensive care settings and the potential for rapid clinical deterioration, there is a need to better characterize the clinical spectrum, management approaches, and outcomes of acute 2,4-D poisoning. The objective of this study was to describe the clinical presentation, intensive care management, and outcomes of eight patients with acute 2,4-D herbicide poisoning managed at a rural tertiary-care center in Central India. To our knowledge, this represents one of the larger contemporary ICU-based series from a rural South Asian setting describing severe 2,4-D toxicity from a rural South Asian intensive care setting.

== Methods ==

This retrospective observational study included eight consecutive patients managed for suspected acute 2,4-dichlorophenoxyacetic acid (2,4-D) poisoning at a tertiary-care rural teaching hospital in Central India over an 18-month period. Cases were identified through emergency department and intensive care unit records based on documented exposure history, container identification when available, and characteristic toxidrome in the absence of confirmatory toxicology.

All eligible consecutive cases during the study period were included to minimize selection bias. Adult and adolescent patients presenting with clinical features compatible with phenoxy herbicide toxicity were included, while cases with uncertain exposure history or incomplete records were excluded.

Demographic characteristics, clinical presentation, laboratory parameters, management strategies, and outcomes were extracted from electronic and paper medical records. Laboratory evaluation included arterial blood gas analysis, renal and liver function tests, serum electrolytes, creatine phosphokinase levels, and electrocardiography. Echocardiography was performed in patients with suspected cardiac involvement. Toxicological assays for serum or urine 2,4-D levels were not available at the study center.

All patients received supportive care according to institutional protocols, including airway protection, hemodynamic support, urinary alkalinization with intravenous sodium bicarbonate when indicated, and renal replacement therapy for severe metabolic derangements or acute kidney injury.

The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee (Ref No. MGIMS/IEC/MED/83/2025), with waiver of informed consent due to the retrospective nature of the study and anonymized data analysis. All procedures conformed to the ethical standards of the Declaration of Helsinki.

== Results ==

== Cohort characteristics ==

A total of eight consecutive patients with suspected acute 2,4-dichlorophenoxyacetic acid (2,4-D) poisoning were included. The cohort consisted of seven males and one female, with ages ranging from 17 to 60 years. Comorbidities were present in two patients, and included diabetes mellitus and hypertension. All exposures were intentional ingestions occurring in rural settings, frequently associated with alcohol consumption. Estimated ingested volumes varied widely, from minimal exposure (<20 mL) to large-volume ingestion (∼250 mL). Time from ingestion to hospital presentation ranged from 0.5 to 3 h.

Clinical severity ranged from isolated gastrointestinal manifestations to fulminant multisystem toxicity. Four patients presented with altered sensorium and hemodynamic instability, while the remaining four patients were initially hemodynamically stable with predominantly gastrointestinal symptoms. All non-survivors presented with hypotension and metabolic acidosis on admission, whereas none of the survivors demonstrated early hemodynamic compromise. Overall, 4/8 (50%) patients required vasopressor support, 2/8 (25%) required dialysis, and 4/8 (50%) died.

== Clinical manifestations and organ involvement ==

Metabolic acidosis was observed in most patients at presentation and represented the most consistent biochemical abnormality. Central nervous system depression was common among non-survivors, with low Glasgow Coma Scale scores on admission. Rhabdomyolysis, evidenced by markedly elevated creatine phosphokinase levels (>5000 IU/L) and cola-colored urine, occurred in two patients and was associated with subsequent acute kidney injury. Acute kidney injury developed in three patients, two of whom required renal replacement therapy (Table 1).

Cardiovascular toxicity was observed in one patient with normal electrolytes who developed inferior wall ST-segment elevation about 2 h after ingestion with echocardiographic confirmation of myocardial dysfunction, progressing to cardiogenic shock within 12 h of admission. Upper gastrointestinal mucosal injury manifested as blood-stained vomiting in one patient, suggesting local irritant effects of the ingested formulation.

== Management ==

All patients received supportive management including intravenous fluids and monitoring. Mechanical ventilation and vasopressor support were required in four patients presenting with severe hypotension and altered sensorium. Urinary alkalinization using intravenous sodium bicarbonate was initiated in patients with moderate-to-severe toxicity with the aim of achieving a target urine pH of > 7.5, where urine pH monitoring was feasible. Renal replacement therapy was performed using intermittent hemodialysis based on institutional protocols for severe metabolic acidosis and acute kidney injury (Table 1). In Cases 2 and 4, urinary alkalinization was initiated but could not be adequately continued due to extremely rapid clinical deterioration with refractory shock. Renal replacement therapy could not be initiated in these patients due to hemodynamic instability

== Outcomes ==

Overall mortality was 50% (4/8 patients). Non-survivors were characterized by early hemodynamic instability, severe metabolic acidosis, and multiorgan involvement at presentation. Descriptive comparison between survivors and non-survivors demonstrated that altered sensorium, hypotension requiring vasopressors, severe metabolic acidosis, and renal dysfunction were observed among non-survivors in this cohort (Table 2). All deaths occurred in patients presenting with hypotension on admission. The median duration of hospital stay among survivors was 4.5 days (IQR: 4–8.5 days), whereas non-survivors had a shorter and more variable hospital stay (median: 2.25 days; IQR: 0.4–5 days), reflecting early mortality due to severe toxicity rather than prolonged disease course. == Discussion ==

In this retrospective observational study of eight patients with acute 2,4-dichlorophenoxyacetic acid poisoning, we identified a spectrum of toxicity ranging from isolated metabolic abnormalities to rapidly progressive multiorgan failure, with an overall mortality of 50%. Severe outcomes were consistently associated with early hemodynamic instability, metabolic acidosis, rhabdomyolysis, and acute kidney injury. These findings reinforce the potential lethality of phenoxy herbicide poisoning and highlight the diagnostic and therapeutic challenges faced in resource-limited rural settings [7], [8], [9]. These observations align with emerging global data emphasizing the continued burden of herbicide self-poisoning in agricultural regions [1]. Similar clinical patterns have been reported in case series from South Asia, where phenoxy herbicide poisoning presents with multisystem involvement and high mortality.

One of the most notable observations in this cohort was the presence of myocardial dysfunction manifesting as ST-segment elevation and echocardiographic abnormalities in a critically ill patient. Cardiac involvement in 2,4-D poisoning remains underrecognized, although chlorophenoxy herbicides are known to disrupt cellular membranes and mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation, potentially leading to myocardial energy failure and ischemia-like electrocardiographic changes [4], [5]. Previous reports have described cardiovascular instability and arrhythmias in severe phenoxy herbicide toxicity, but detailed clinical descriptions remain limited [6]. The observed cardiogenic shock in the absence of electrolyte abnormalities in our cohort suggests the possibility of direct myocardial toxicity rather than primary coronary occlusion. This is biologically plausible, as chlorophenoxy herbicides are known to impair mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation, leading to myocardial energy failure and ischemia-like electrocardiographic changes [5].

The clinical presentation of 2,4-D poisoning frequently overlaps with organophosphate or carbamate toxicity, creating diagnostic uncertainty during early evaluation [7], [8]. Features such as miosis, altered sensorium, and hypotension may mimic cholinergic toxidrome. However, classical muscarinic features such as excessive salivation, lacrimation, bronchorrhea, and bradycardia were not consistently observed in our cohort, providing a useful clinical distinction from organophosphate poisoning in resource-limited settings. Recognition of distinguishing features including severe metabolic acidosis, rhabdomyolysis, and neurological depression is also essential to avoid inappropriate management strategies.

Multiorgan involvement in this cohort was consistent with previously described mechanisms of phenoxy herbicide toxicity. Experimental and clinical studies suggest that 2,4-D induces cellular injury through uncoupling of oxidative phosphorylation, oxidative stress, and disruption of acetyl-coenzyme A metabolism, resulting in skeletal muscle, renal tubular, and neuronal injury [4], [5]. Rhabdomyolysis observed in several patients likely reflected both direct myotoxic effects and secondary ischemic injury during shock, contributing to the development of acute kidney injury and worsening metabolic acidosis. The presence of cola-colored urine and markedly elevated creatine phosphokinase levels provided important clinical clues to underlying muscle toxicity.

Management of 2,4-D poisoning remains largely supportive, as no specific antidote exists. Urinary alkalinization has been proposed to enhance renal elimination of weak acids such as 2,4-D by promoting ion trapping within renal tubules [9], [10]. In our cohort, urinary alkalinization was used in patients with moderate-to-severe toxicity, although the retrospective design precludes definitive conclusions regarding its efficacy. Similarly, renal replacement therapy was instituted in patients with severe metabolic derangements or acute kidney injury, consistent with previously reported clinical practice [9], [10].

An important observation in this study was the apparent relationship between estimated ingestion volume and clinical severity. Patients with minimal exposure demonstrated milder disease and favorable outcomes, whereas larger or unknown ingestions were associated with early shock and mortality. Notably, ingestion volume was undocumented in several non-survivors, which in rural settings may reflect unobserved or large-volume intentional exposures, potentially correlating with more severe toxicity.

However, individual variability in metabolism, delayed presentation, and co-ingestion of alcohol or other pesticides may have influenced clinical trajectories, underscoring the complexity of predicting outcomes in herbicide poisoning.

This study has several limitations. Toxicological confirmation using serum or urine 2,4-D levels was not available, and diagnosis relied on clinical history and identification of ingested formulations, which may introduce misclassification bias [11]. The small sample size and retrospective design limit the ability to establish causal relationships or evaluate treatment efficacy. Important variables such as ingestion volume, comorbidities, and detailed serial clinical progression were inconsistently documented due to the retrospective design, which may influence outcome interpretation.

Nonetheless, the inclusion of consecutive patients from a rural tertiary care center provides valuable real-world insights into the clinical spectrum of 2,4-D toxicity in regions where advanced toxicological testing is unavailable.

== Clinical implications ==

Acute 2,4-dichlorophenoxyacetic acid poisoning should be considered in patients presenting with unexplained metabolic acidosis, altered sensorium, or rhabdomyolysis, particularly in agricultural settings where herbicide exposure is common. Severe cases may involve myocardial dysfunction, highlighting the importance of early electrocardiographic and echocardiographic evaluation in critically ill patients. Management remains largely supportive, with urinary alkalinization and intensive care measures forming the cornerstone of treatment, although further prospective studies are needed to clarify their impact on outcomes. Early recognition of phenoxy herbicide toxicity is essential to avoid misdiagnosis as organophosphate poisoning and prevent inappropriate therapy. In rural intensive care units where toxicological assays are often unavailable, maintaining a high index of clinical suspicion is crucial for timely diagnosis and management. Future multicenter prospective studies are needed to validate these findings and establish standardized management strategies for phenoxy herbicide poisoning.

== Conclusion ==

Acute 2,4-dichlorophenoxyacetic acid herbicide poisoning demonstrated a broad clinical spectrum in this rural observational cohort, ranging from mild gastrointestinal illness to rapidly progressive multiorgan dysfunction with substantial mortality. Early identification of high-risk features such as hypotension, severe metabolic acidosis, central nervous system depression, and organ involvement were associated with poor outcomes, while myocardial dysfunction observed in one patient suggests that cardiac toxicity may be an underrecognized manifestation of phenoxy herbicide exposure. In the absence of specific antidotes and limited access to toxicological assays, timely recognition and aggressive supportive management remain central to care. These findings contribute to the limited clinical literature on 2,4-D toxicity from resource-limited settings and underscore the need for increased clinical awareness and further multicenter studies to better define prognostic indicators and optimize management strategies.
